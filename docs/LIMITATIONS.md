# Limitations and foreseeable failure modes

SILENCE ENGINE is an early-warning and decision-support prototype. Read every output as a question to verify, not an answer.

## About the evidence

1. **Synthetic evaluation only.** All metrics come from a generator written by the same team that wrote the triage rules. They show the logic behaves as designed under noise, missing data and reporting delays; they do not measure real-world accuracy. Real services will have patterns, failure modes and context signals the generator does not imagine.
2. **Small samples.** The test period has 55 alertable and 38 benign events. 95% Wilson intervals are wide (engine recall 74–92%, explanation accuracy 75–94%). Differences of a few points between methods are not meaningful.
3. **Absence of activity is not harm.** A shortfall in *recorded* activity can mean reduced need, people using another location, a booking-system change, or a reporting artefact. The engine never infers that any person was denied a service.
4. **Coincidence is not cause.** Weather, transport and outage signals are shown because they overlap in time. No causal effect is estimated.
5. **Covert reporting failures are invisible.** If a data fault leaves feed metadata intact (for example an export that silently drops one category), it looks exactly like a real decline and will be triaged as a potential access gap. The synthetic data includes such cases on purpose; they are mis-classified by every method.
6. **Feed metadata matters.** Heartbeat, upload batches and last-seen times make the reporting-vs-access distinction possible. A CSV with counts only can still separate *missing* from *zero*, but not subtler failures.

## About the method

7. **Low-volume services are harder.** With ~7–15 events per day, a 40% drop is within ordinary noise for several days. Synthetic recall is 67% for low-volume streams vs 100% for high-volume streams. The CUSUM helps with persistent declines; aggregating to weeks would help more (future work). Priority is volume-neutral so small services are not pushed down the queue, but they still alert less often.
8. **Baselines lag.** The same-weekday median over 8 weeks adapts slowly to genuine trend or seasonal change (up to ~7% bias at the steepest synthetic seasonal slope), and a very long gap (more than half of the 8-week window) starts to contaminate its own baseline.
9. **Schedules are booleans.** Reduced opening hours, partial closures and special events are not modelled; they appear as declines unless the schedule marks the day closed.
10. **Inferred schedules.** When no record arrives, the schedule is inferred from the usual weekly pattern; a holiday during a feed outage would be mistaken for a missing open day.
11. **Fixed triage thresholds.** Only three detection thresholds are tuned; triage cut-offs (35%/20% shortfall, feed 60%/20%) are design choices documented in METHODOLOGY.md, not learned. (An optional ML benchmark learns them from synthetic examples; see items 19–22.)
12. **Scores are heuristics.** Priority and evidence sufficiency are transparent formulas, not probabilities; they were not calibrated against real outcomes.
13. **Delays.** Retrospective delay figures assume each record arrives on its service day. The operational replay shows the cost of real delays (median +1 day with a one-day grace period). Records more than 7 days late would break the replay's assumption that history is complete.
14. **Context coverage is uneven.** Sites with richer context feeds will look "better explained" than sites without them. Missing context is reported as "cause unobserved", never as "no cause".

## About the system

15. **SQLite and one process.** Designed for a single gunicorn worker with threads; background jobs are held in memory and are lost on restart. Not load-tested.
16. **Authentication is minimal.** Loopback-only writes or a single shared bearer token; no user accounts or roles. Suitable for a demo over an SSH tunnel, not for multi-user production.
17. **Imported data is not evaluated.** Without ground truth there are no accuracy figures for imported data; thresholds tuned on synthetic data may not suit it.
18. **Not deployed on s390x yet by the authors.** The deployment kit is ready and the code is architecture-neutral, but results on LinuxONE must be confirmed by running it (see IBM_Z_DEPLOYMENT.md). This includes the optional ML benchmark: it was run only on macOS arm64 (identical hashes with scikit-learn 1.5.1 and 1.7.2), not on s390x.

## About the optional ML triage benchmark

19. **A small, synthetic training set.** The model learns from 42 flagged episodes (access gap 16, reporting problem 15, uncertain 11) generated by the same team's generator, and is scored on 50. One episode moves accuracy by two percentage points. The reported 88% vs 87% is not a difference; the paired exact McNemar p is 1.00. Nothing here shows that ML triage is better, and no claim should say so.
20. **Same evidence as the rules.** The model reads the same per-episode evidence the rules threshold (it is never given their verdict) and its most important features are the signals the rules threshold (the share of missing or suspect days and the strength of the decline), so it largely re-learns the rules. It cannot find information the features do not contain: a covert reporting failure looks like a real decline to both methods.
21. **It cannot change detection.** The detector decides what is flagged. The model only chooses a category for flagged episodes, so it cannot raise recall or remove false alerts, and scheduled-closure episodes stay under the schedule rule. Episodes of kinds absent from the training period (for example normal variation that the detector flagged) cannot be predicted.
22. **Labels come from a generator.** On real data there would be no such labels; the model would have to learn from reviewers' recorded outcomes, which are few, delayed and biased toward the cases the rules surfaced first. Until then it is an experiment, not a deployable classifier, and the dashboard keeps the rule-based triage as the default.
23. **Library versions and platforms.** Results were identical across two scikit-learn/NumPy versions on arm64, but floating-point and library differences can in principle change a split on another platform. `predictions_sha256` (categories only) is the stricter check to compare on s390x; record both library versions if it differs.

## Failure modes to watch for in real use

| Failure | Effect | Mitigation in this prototype |
|---|---|---|
| Unequal reporting coverage between sites | Sites with flaky feeds generate more "reporting" cases; their real gaps may hide behind them | Feed health shown separately; "uncertain" when a decline coincides with a degraded feed |
| A whole region's feed fails | Many simultaneous reporting cases | Peer comparison reports how many neighbours are affected |
| Analysts treat priority as probability | Over-confidence | Scores labelled as heuristics; formula shown on every case |
| Alerts used punitively against a site | Harm to staff and service users | Human review required; language frames cases as hypotheses; no automatic actions |
| Baseline drift after a long outage | Under- or over-alerting afterwards | Distrusted days excluded from baselines; insufficient history surfaces as "uncertain" |
